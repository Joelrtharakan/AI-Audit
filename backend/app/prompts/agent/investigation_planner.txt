QUESTION QUALITY — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PART:
A question is only useful if it is:
  (1) SINGLE DECISION: Evaluates exactly ONE decision point. Never combine multiple independent checks (BAD: "Was X required AND did they do it?").
  (2) SPECIFIC: Names the actual object/record/period from THIS finding — not a generic category.
  (3) PURPOSEFUL: States which proposition / unknown it resolves (the "purpose" / "objective" field).
  (4) EVIDENCE-TARGETED: Names the specific document/record type that would answer it (the "evidence" field).
  (5) CONDITIONAL STRUCTURE: If a question depends on an antecedent outcome, specify "activation_condition" and "depends_on".

If the finding text ITSELF states competing causal mechanisms ("could have resulted from A, B,
or C"), you MUST include a P1 discrimination question naming those mechanisms and asking for the
objective evidence (contemporaneous execution/transaction records, system audit trail and
timestamps, independent verification/recount, secondary records) that would be uniquely
consistent with ONE mechanism and not the others. Do not replace it with generic questions.

Priority rules:
  P1: Resolve direct evidence conflicts.
  P2: Establish whether the alleged deviation/event actually occurred.
  P3: Establish applicable requirement/control.
  P4: Establish operational consequence / hypothesis discrimination.
  P5: Identify causal mechanism (never before event/requirement is established).
  P6: Investigate systemic/recurrence cause.

BAD example: "Was acknowledgement mandatory and was it completed?" — Double question.
GOOD example: "Did the applicable procedure require acknowledgement before work commenced?" — Single decision.
Then, conditional: "Do authenticated records establish that affected personnel acknowledged the revision before activity occurred?" — Activation condition: "If acknowledgement was required".

- Questions must NOT ask for facts already established in the finding text.
- Do NOT ask about training, authorization, or communication unless the finding explicitly mentions them.
